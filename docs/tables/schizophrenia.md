# 3.2.1 — Schizophrenia or Schizophreniform Disorder

**Schizophrenia / Schizophreniform Disorder:** Two or more active-phase symptoms (delusions, hallucinations, disorganized speech, grossly disorganized/catatonic behavior, negative symptoms) with functional decline; ≥6 months for schizophrenia, 1–6 months for schizophreniform.

| Consider and rule out | How it is distinguished from Schizophrenia / Schizophreniform Disorder |
| --- | --- |
| [Schizoaffective Disorder](3-2-2.md) | A mood episode is present for the majority of the total illness duration, with ≥2 weeks of psychosis without prominent mood symptoms. |
| [Bipolar/Depressive Disorder With Psychotic Features](3-4-1.md) | Psychotic symptoms occur exclusively during mood episodes. |
| [Delusional Disorder](3-2-3.md) | Delusions occur without the other active-phase symptoms and functioning is otherwise relatively preserved. |
| [Brief Psychotic Disorder](3-2-4.md) | Duration is at least 1 day but less than 1 month with return to premorbid functioning. |
| Substance/Medication-Induced Psychotic Disorder | Psychosis is attributable to a substance and typically remits with sustained abstinence. |
| Psychotic Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition | Psychosis is the direct physiological consequence of a medical condition. |
| [Autism Spectrum Disorder](3-1-3.md) | Prominent delusions/hallucinations are absent; social-communication deficits date from early development. |
| [Schizotypal Personality Disorder](3-17-3.md) | Attenuated, subthreshold psychotic-like features without frank psychosis. |
