# 3.5.2 — Selective Mutism

**Selective Mutism:** Consistent failure to speak in specific social situations where speaking is expected, despite speaking in others, interfering with functioning.

| Consider and rule out | How it is distinguished from Selective Mutism |
| --- | --- |
| [Communication Disorders](3-1-2.md) | Speech difficulty is not restricted to specific social situations. |
| [Autism Spectrum Disorder](3-1-3.md) | Failure to speak reflects social-communication deficits present across settings. |
| [Social Anxiety Disorder](3-5-4.md) | Broader social fear; selective mutism specifically involves not speaking and may be diagnosed alongside it. |
| Normal shyness / adjustment (e.g., new language) | Transient silence during adjustment to a new environment or language does not warrant the diagnosis. |

---
_Distinctions summarize differentiating features only. Confirm the full DSM-5 criteria for each disorder considered._
