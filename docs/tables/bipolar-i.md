# 3.3.1 — Bipolar I Disorder

**Bipolar I Disorder:** At least one Manic Episode (≥1 week or hospitalization, with marked impairment or psychosis), usually with major depressive episodes.

| Consider and rule out | How it is distinguished from Bipolar I Disorder |
| --- | --- |
| [Bipolar II Disorder](3-3-2.md) | Hypomanic (not full manic) episodes plus major depressive episodes; never a full manic episode. |
| [Major Depressive Disorder](3-4-1.md) | No lifetime history of a manic or hypomanic episode. |
| [Cyclothymic Disorder](3-3-3.md) | Chronic subthreshold hypomanic and depressive symptoms that never meet full episode criteria. |
| [Schizoaffective Disorder](3-2-2.md) | ≥2 weeks of psychosis occur without a mood episode. |
| Substance/Medication-Induced Bipolar Disorder | Mood elevation is attributable to a substance/medication and does not persist beyond its physiological effect. |
| Bipolar Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition | Mania is the direct physiological consequence of a medical condition. |
| [ADHD](3-1-4.md) | Chronic, non-episodic overactivity/impulsivity without discrete mood episodes (may co-occur, especially in youth). |
| [Borderline Personality Disorder](3-17-5.md) | Mood shifts are rapid, reactive to interpersonal events, and lack sustained episodes with increased energy/decreased sleep. |

_Distinctions summarize differentiating features only. Confirm the full DSM-5 criteria for each disorder considered._
