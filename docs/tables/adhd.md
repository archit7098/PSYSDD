# 3.1.4 — Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder

**Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder:** Persistent inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity, onset before age 12, present in ≥2 settings and impairing functioning.

| Consider and rule out | How it is distinguished from Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder |
| --- | --- |
| [Oppositional Defiant Disorder](3-14-1.md) | Resistance to tasks reflects opposition to authority rather than an inability to sustain attention. |
| [Specific Learning Disorder](3-1-5.md) | Inattentive appearance is limited to academically demanding tasks and stems from skill deficits. |
| [Intellectual Disability](3-1-1.md) | Symptoms are commensurate with mental age; ADHD diagnosed additionally if inattention/hyperactivity exceed developmental level. |
| [Bipolar Disorder](3-3-1.md) | Inattention/overactivity is episodic and accompanied by mood elevation, whereas ADHD is chronic and non-episodic. |
| [Disruptive Mood Dysregulation Disorder](3-4-4.md) | Characterized by persistent irritability and severe temper outbursts beyond the impulsivity of ADHD. |
| [Anxiety Disorders](3-5-7.md) | Inattention is due to worry/rumination rather than distractibility by external stimuli. |
| [Substance Use Disorders](3-15-1.md) | Inattention began with substance use rather than in childhood. |

---
_Distinctions summarize differentiating features only. Confirm the full DSM-5 criteria for each disorder considered._
