# 3.4.1 — Major Depressive Disorder

**Major Depressive Disorder:** One or more Major Depressive Episodes (≥2 weeks of depressed mood/anhedonia plus additional symptoms) without a history of mania/hypomania.

| Consider and rule out | How it is distinguished from Major Depressive Disorder |
| --- | --- |
| [Bipolar I/II Disorder](3-3-1.md) | A lifetime manic or hypomanic episode has occurred. |
| [Persistent Depressive Disorder](3-4-2.md) | Chronic depressed mood for ≥2 years, often at lower symptom intensity. |
| [Adjustment Disorder With Depressed Mood](3-7-2.md) | Depressive symptoms follow a stressor and do not meet full major depressive episode criteria. |
| Depressive Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition | Depression is the direct physiological result of a medical condition. |
| Substance/Medication-Induced Depressive Disorder | Depression is attributable to a substance/medication. |
| [Schizoaffective Disorder](3-2-2.md) | Psychosis occurs for ≥2 weeks outside of mood episodes. |
| Uncomplicated bereavement / normal sadness | A normative reaction to loss that does not reflect an internal dysfunction (clinical judgment required). |
| [ADHD / Demoralization](3-1-4.md) | Poor concentration or low mood is secondary to functional impairment without a full depressive syndrome. |

---
_Distinctions summarize differentiating features only. Confirm the full DSM-5 criteria for each disorder considered._
