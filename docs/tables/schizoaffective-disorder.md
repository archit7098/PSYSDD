# 3.2.2 — Schizoaffective Disorder

**Schizoaffective Disorder:** An uninterrupted illness with a major mood episode concurrent with active-phase schizophrenia symptoms, plus ≥2 weeks of delusions/hallucinations without a major mood episode, with mood episodes present for the majority of the illness.

| Consider and rule out | How it is distinguished from Schizoaffective Disorder |
| --- | --- |
| [Schizophrenia](3-2-1.md) | Mood episodes, if present, occupy only a minority of the total active and residual illness. |
| [Major Depressive/Bipolar Disorder With Psychotic Features](3-4-1.md) | Psychotic symptoms occur exclusively during mood episodes (no ≥2-week period of psychosis without mood symptoms). |
| Substance/Medication-Induced Psychotic Disorder | The combined mood and psychotic picture is attributable to a substance. |
| Psychotic Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition | Symptoms are the physiological consequence of a medical condition. |

---
_Distinctions summarize differentiating features only. Confirm the full DSM-5 criteria for each disorder considered._
